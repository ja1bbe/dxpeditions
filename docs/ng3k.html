Map and “suggested bands and times” (under test): positions are entity centres from cty.dat and may differ from the actual site by a few hundred km. The physics guide uses the same rules as JA1BBE Space Weather Nowcast: low bands when most of the path is dark (sun below −6°), using the long path when the short path crosses the auroral oval or is mostly sunlit; mid bands when the path mixes day and night; high bands when most of the path is sunlit and SFI is adequate. Active operations: next 24 hours, including the current auroral oval. Upcoming operations: sun positions on the start date. This is not a MUF prediction, and 6 m is not covered. When your station is in Japan, hours when Japanese stations heard the entity on PSK Reporter (in at least 30% of samples; nearby grid square if the entity has no record) are also shown, and hours where the record and physics agree are marked “Best bet”.
Map overlays are for the current time: auroral oval from NOAA OVATION (30–90 min forecast), D-region absorption from NOAA D-RAP, storm extent estimated from solar-wind Bz and speed (NOAA RTSW; under test), and geomagnetic latitude from a dipole approximation. A skewed heading is a hint for a path bending around the auroral oval, not a guarantee. A callsign is placed at its entity centre. Text appears in Japanese when your station is in Japan, otherwise in English.
Map: Natural Earth (land.json). Space weather: NOAA SWPC. Reception record: PSK Reporter data compiled by JA1BBE (same as Space Weather Nowcast).
